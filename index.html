Stochastic calculus for trading agents

Ito Agentics

We build trading agents from mathematics. Each agent runs one method. Each method is an equation.

\mathrm{d}f(t,X_t)=\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\mu_t\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_t^{2}\,\frac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\brk+\sigma_t\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,\mathrm{d}W_t
Itô's lemma, 1951
01

Stochastic calculus

Prices move at random. Kiyosi Itô built the calculus for random motion: how to integrate against a path that is too rough to differentiate.

Brownian motion
W_0=0,\qquad W_t-W_s\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,t-s),\qquad W_t-W_s\ \perp\!\!\!\perp\ \mathcal{F}_s

The model of pure noise. Each step is independent of the past and normally distributed.

Itô integral
\int_0^T H_t\,\mathrm{d}W_t=\lim_{\lVert\Pi\rVert\to 0}\ \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}H_{t_i}\left(W_{t_{i+1}}-W_{t_i}\right)

The position H is chosen at the start of each step, before the move is known. That is what makes it a trading gain.

Quadratic variation
[W,W]_t=\lim_{\lVert\Pi\rVert\to 0}\sum_{i}\left(W_{t_{i+1}}-W_{t_i}\right)^{2}=t,\qquad (\mathrm{d}W_t)^{2}=\mathrm{d}t

The squared steps of a Brownian path add up to elapsed time. Ordinary calculus has no such term.

Itô isometry
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\int_0^T H_t\,\mathrm{d}W_t\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^T H_t^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

The variance of a trading gain equals the expected sum of squared positions.

Geometric Brownian motion
\mathrm{d}S_t=\mu S_t\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma S_t\,\mathrm{d}W_t\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ S_t=S_0\exp\!\left(\left(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\right)t+\sigma W_t\right)

The standard model of a stock price. The lines drawn behind this page are sample paths of it.

Stratonovich correction
\int_0^T X_t\circ\mathrm{d}W_t=\int_0^T X_t\,\mathrm{d}W_t+\frac{1}{2}\,[X,W]_T

Two ways to define the integral. They differ by half the covariation.

Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process
\mathrm{d}X_t=\theta(\mu-X_t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_t,\quad X_t=\mu+(X_0-\mu)e^{-\theta t}+\sigma\!\int_0^t e^{-\theta(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}W_s

Noise with a pull back toward a mean. The base model for mean reversion.

Time change (Dambis, Dubins–Schwarz)
M_t=B_{[M,M]_t}

Every continuous martingale is a Brownian motion run on its own clock.

Euler–Maruyama scheme
X_{n+1}=X_n+\mu(X_n)\,\Delta t+\sigma(X_n)\sqrt{\Delta t}\;Z_n,\qquad Z_n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)

How a computer simulates a stochastic differential equation, one step at a time.

02

Pricing and diffusion

Random paths and partial differential equations describe the same thing from two sides. An expected value over paths solves an equation, and the reverse.

Black–Scholes equation
\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}S^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial S^{2}}+rS\frac{\partial V}{\partial S}-rV=0

The price V of an option on a stock S must satisfy this equation.

Black–Scholes formula
C=S_0\,\Phi(d_1)-Ke^{-rT}\,\Phi(d_2),\qquad d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(S_0/K)+\left(r\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\right)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}

Its solution for a call option with strike K and expiry T.

Feynman–Kac formula
u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,e^{-\int_t^T r(X_s)\,\mathrm{d}s}\,g(X_T)\ \middle|\ X_t=x\right]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+\mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}-ru=0,\quad u(T,x)=g(x)

A discounted expected payoff over random paths is the solution of a differential equation.

Fokker–Planck equation
\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big[\mu(x,t)\,p\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\Big[\sigma^{2}(x,t)\,p\Big]

How the probability density p of a diffusing quantity changes over time.

Girsanov theorem
\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}\right|_{\mathcal{F}_T}=\exp\!\left(-\int_0^T\theta_s\,\mathrm{d}W_s-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^T\theta_s^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right),\quad \widetilde{W}_t=W_t+\int_0^t\theta_s\,\mathrm{d}s

Reweighting the probabilities removes the drift. The shifted process is a Brownian motion under the new measure.

03

Optimization and control

An agent chooses how much to hold at each moment. Control theory finds the choice that is best over the whole horizon.

Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation
0=\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}+\sup_{u}\left\{\,f(x,u)+\mu(x,u)\,\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma^{2}(x,u)\,\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial x^{2}}\right\},\qquad V(T,x)=g(x)

V is the best total reward reachable from state x at time t. The control u that attains the supremum is the optimal action.

Merton fraction
\pi^{*}=\frac{\mu-r}{\gamma\,\sigma^{2}}

The share of wealth to hold in a risky asset, for an investor with risk aversion γ.

Kelly criterion
f^{*}=\underset{f}{\arg\max}\ \mathbb{E}\big[\log(1+fR)\big]

The bet size that grows wealth fastest over many repeated bets with return R.

Mean–variance portfolio
\max_{w}\ \ w^{\top}(\mu-r\mathbf{1})-\frac{\gamma}{2}\,w^{\top}\Sigma\,w\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ w^{*}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\Sigma^{-1}(\mu-r\mathbf{1})

Weights w that trade expected return against variance, given the covariance matrix Σ.

Kalman filter
K_t=P_{t|t-1}H^{\top}\!\left(HP_{t|t-1}H^{\top}+R\right)^{-1},\quad \hat{x}_{t|t}=\hat{x}_{t|t-1}+K_t\left(y_t-H\hat{x}_{t|t-1}\right)

Updates an estimate of a hidden state each time a noisy measurement y arrives.

04

Path signatures

The signature turns a whole price path into a list of numbers: its iterated integrals. Those numbers describe the shape of the path and the order in which things happened.

Signature of a path
S(X)_{s,t}=\bigg(1,\ \int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{d}X_{u},\quad \int_{s\lt u_1\lt u_2\lt t}\mathrm{d}X_{u_1}\otimes\mathrm{d}X_{u_2},\quad \ldots,\ \int_{s\lt u_1\lt\cdots\lt u_k\lt t}\mathrm{d}X_{u_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{d}X_{u_k},\ \ \ldots\bigg)

Level one is the total move. Level two holds the areas swept out. Each further level adds finer detail.

Chen's identity
S(X)_{s,u}\otimes S(X)_{u,t}=S(X)_{s,t}

Joining two pieces of a path multiplies their signatures.

Shuffle product
\big\langle S(X),\,e_I\big\rangle\,\big\langle S(X),\,e_J\big\rangle=\big\langle S(X),\ e_I\,\sqcup\!\sqcup\,e_J\big\rangle

A product of two signature terms is a sum of signature terms. Linear models on signatures can therefore fit nonlinear functions of the path.

Lévy area
A_{s,t}=\frac{1}{2}\int_s^t\left(X^{1}_u-X^{1}_s\right)\mathrm{d}X^{2}_u-\left(X^{2}_u-X^{2}_s\right)\mathrm{d}X^{1}_u

The signed area between a two-dimensional path and its chord. It records which coordinate moved first.

05

Random matrix theory

A correlation matrix measured from limited data is mostly noise. Random matrix theory says exactly what pure noise looks like, so the signal can be told apart from it.

Marchenko–Pastur law
\rho(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{(\lambda_{+}-\lambda)(\lambda-\lambda_{-})}}{2\pi\,q\,\sigma^{2}\lambda},\qquad \lambda_{\pm}=\sigma^{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{q}\right)^{2},\quad q=\frac{N}{T}

The eigenvalue density of a covariance matrix of N unrelated series over T observations. Eigenvalues above the upper edge carry information.

Wigner semicircle law
\rho(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\sqrt{4\sigma^{2}-x^{2}},\qquad |x|\le 2\sigma

The eigenvalue density of a large symmetric matrix with random entries.

Stieltjes transform
G(z)=\int\frac{\rho(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\qquad \rho(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\operatorname{Im}\,G(\lambda-i\varepsilon)

The tool that turns a question about eigenvalues into an algebraic equation.

06

Topology

Topological data analysis measures the shape of data: clusters, loops and voids, and how long each one survives as the scale changes.

Homology
\partial_{k}\circ\partial_{k+1}=0,\qquad H_k=\ker\partial_k\,\big/\,\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1},\qquad \beta_k=\operatorname{rank}H_k

The boundary of a boundary is empty. The Betti number β counts the holes of dimension k.

Vietoris–Rips complex
\mathrm{VR}_{\varepsilon}(X)=\big\{\,\sigma\subseteq X\ :\ d(x,y)\le\varepsilon\ \ \text{for all }x,y\in\sigma\,\big\}

Joins every group of data points that lie within distance ε of each other.

Persistent homology
H_k^{\,i,j}=\operatorname{im}\!\Big(H_k(K_i)\longrightarrow H_k(K_j)\Big),\qquad i\le j

The holes present at scale i that still exist at scale j.

Stability theorem
d_B\big(\mathrm{Dgm}(f),\,\mathrm{Dgm}(g)\big)\le\lVert f-g\rVert_{\infty}

A small change in the data moves the persistence diagram by at most as much.

Euler characteristic
\chi=\sum_{k\ge 0}(-1)^{k}\beta_k

One number that summarises the holes of every dimension.

07

Time series

The statistics of data that arrives in order, one bar at a time.

Moving average crossover
\mathrm{SMA}_n(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}P_{t-i},\qquad e_t=\mathbf{1}\big\{\mathrm{SMA}_{\text{fast}}(t)\gt\mathrm{SMA}_{\text{slow}}(t)\big\}

Hold the asset while the short average of price P sits above the long one.

GARCH(1,1)
\sigma_t^{2}=\omega+\alpha\,\varepsilon_{t-1}^{2}+\beta\,\sigma_{t-1}^{2}

Volatility clusters. A large move yesterday raises the expected variance today.

Autoregression
X_t=c+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\varphi_i\,X_{t-i}+\varepsilon_t

Today's value as a weighted sum of the last p values plus noise.

Sharpe ratio
\mathrm{SR}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[R]-r_f}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(R)}}

Return earned above the risk-free rate, per unit of risk taken.

Bayes' rule
p(\theta\mid x)=\frac{p(x\mid\theta)\,p(\theta)}{\int p(x\mid\theta')\,p(\theta')\,\mathrm{d}\theta'}

How evidence x updates belief about an unknown θ.

Maximum drawdown
\mathrm{MDD}=\max_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\frac{\max_{s\le t}V_s-V_t}{\max_{s\le t}V_s}\right)

The largest fall of account value V from a previous peak.
